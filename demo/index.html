What does skewness tell us?

Skewness describes the asymmetry of a distribution through its third central moment divided by its variance to the power 3/2. Here, even after the specified uncertainties, the admissible ranges for the two intensity laws do not overlap.

For the conditional Poisson model, the corresponding factorial-cumulant ratio removes fixed positive gain and fixed Poisson background. The intervals show mathematical bounds, not measured samples or a confidence level for the proof.

The boundary of the result

The assumptions travel with the bound.

Geometry & motion

  • Exact fixed 63-point mean configurations and permanent spring links.
  • Each fixed edge weight lies in 1 ± 1/100001.
  • Stationary centered Gaussian weighted-spring Ornstein–Uhlenbeck motion.
  • Each strength β lies in [B/2, B], with B ≈ 1.3986078635588654 × 10⁻¹¹.

Exposure & observation

  • Fixed frequency error from π/3 at most 10⁻¹⁴.
  • Positive rectangular exposure ≤ 0.001; window-start lag ≥ 1000, both dimensionless.
  • Counts conditionally independent given the full trajectory, with a Poisson response.
  • Fixed integrated normalized gain in [0.9, 1.1] and background in [0, 1].

What remains open. Physical feasibility and empirical validation are not established. Position errors, drift, nonstationary preparation and non-Poisson response are outside these displayed exact-geometry contracts. The exposure cap 0.01 remains unresolved for exact geometry and has no allocated count.

The proof bounds dependence between windows; it does not assume marginal independence. Fixed integrated gain supplies no physical rate, time or dose. The separate, narrower calibrated iid count result uses a different model and is not displayed here.

Parameter choices were informed by earlier bounds. No novelty, optimality or full-theorem proof-assistant verification is claimed. The displayed decimals are approximate; the certificate uses exact rational and outward interval arithmetic.
